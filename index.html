HISTORY MODULE

Indian History

India’s history spans over 5,000 years, moving from the ancient Indus Valley Civilization and the Vedic age to the classical Mauryan and Gupta golden ages, and later the medieval Islamic and Mughal periods.

Ancient India

  • Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500–1900 BCE): One of the world’s first urban societies, featuring planned cities like Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro.
  • Vedic Period (c. 1500–500 BCE): Indo-Aryan migration brought Sanskrit, the composition of the Vedas, and the roots of early social and philosophical structures.
  • Religions and Empires (6th century BCE–6th century CE): Buddhism and Jainism emerged; Chandragupta Maurya founded the centralized Mauryan Empire, which peaked under Emperor Ashoka.
  • Gupta Golden Age (4th–6th centuries CE): A high point in science, mathematics (including early concepts of zero and pi), literature, and classical art.

Medieval & Sultanate Period

  • Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526): Turkic and Afghan dynasties established Islamic rule across large parts of northern India, shaping Indo-Islamic architecture and culture.
  • Regional Kingdoms: Down south, powerful dynasties like the Cholas, Chalukyas, Pallavas, and later the Vijayanagara Empire flourished and built massive temple complexes.
  • Mughal Empire (1526–1857): Founded by Babur and expanded by rulers like Akbar and Shah Jahan, the empire unified much of the subcontinent and produced iconic monuments like the Taj Mahal.

British Rule & Independence

  • Colonial Expansion (18th–19th centuries): Following the decline of the Mughals and the Battle of Plassey in 1757, the British East India Company gradually seized control, shifting to direct colonial rule.
  • Independence Movement (early–mid 20th century): Led by figures like Mahatma Gandhi, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, and Jawaharlal Nehru, mass civil disobedience forced the end of British rule.
  • Partition and Republic (1947–present): British India was partitioned into independent India and Pakistan in August 1947. India adopted its constitution in 1950 and later liberalized its economy.

World History

Human history is a long journey from the formation of Earth to the modern age, shaped by civilizations, religions, war, science, and change.

1. Cosmic & Deep History

  • The Origin of the Earth: The planet begins as a lifeless mass spinning through space, slowly cooling down over millions of years.
  • The Rise of Life: Life emerges in primeval oceans, evolving from simple microorganisms into fish, amphibians, reptiles, and eventually giant dinosaurs.
  • The Age of Mammals: After the dinosaurs vanish, mammals become dominant, adapting to shifting climates and ice ages until the first early human species appear.

2. The Dawn of Humanity & Early Empires

  • The Neolithic Revolution: Primitive humans transition from nomadic hunting to settled agriculture, developing language, tools, and basic social customs.
  • Cradles of Civilization: The first major organized societies emerge along fertile river valleys, notably in Sumeria (Mesopotamia) and Egypt, introducing writing, math, and formal priesthoods.
  • The Classical Worlds: Power shifts toward the Mediterranean and Asia. Ancient Greece introduces philosophy and democratic ideals, while Alexander the Great rapidly spreads Hellenistic culture.

3. Faiths and Fractured Empires

  • The Rise of Global Religions: The era sees the birth and rapid expansion of major spiritual movements that reshape human morality, including Buddhism in India, Confucianism in China, and Christianity in the Mediterranean.
  • The Roman Empire: Rome rises to dominate the Western world with immense administrative and military power, only to collapse due to internal decay and barbarian invasions, plunging Europe into a period of upheaval.
  • The Islamic Conquests: A dynamic new civilization emerges from Arabia, uniting vast territories across the Middle East, North Africa, and Spain through trade, science, and faith.

4. The Path to the Modern Age

  • The Renaissance & Exploration: Europe awakens from the Middle Ages, sparked by a rebirth of science and art. Navigators cross the oceans, connecting the Americas to the global network.
  • The Industrial & Scientific Revolutions: The invention of the steam engine and automated machinery fundamentally rewrites human economics, shifting populations into cities and sparking modern industry.
  • The Great War: Tensions from imperial competition boil over into World War I, devastating Europe and destroying old empires.
